## Authority, persistence, retention and rollback

- **Authority:** follow the `events-api.js` pattern. Identity comes from `authorized()` (origin, CSRF, waitlist, ban). Wire it through both `app.js` (memory) and `distributed-app.js` (Redis gateway), add a `vercel-routing.js`/`vercel.json` allowlist entry and a `redis-security.js` scope, and use one per-owner document with a compare-and-set revision. The grant is a single scoped, expiring, revocable "`event_plan` draft" grant whose revision bumps on revoke. It is rechecked at each checkpoint and at commit.
- **Execution:** the fixture runs on the service, outside render and world ticks. It holds a lease with a fencing token. Commit is a compare-and-set on the task revision. An expired lease is recovered lazily on the next authorized read, with at most 3 attempts, then it fails closed. Hosting is Vercel functions, so a long-running background worker is **not** available there. That's acceptable for a fast deterministic fixture. It is a **blocking decision for any remote provider** (deferred).
- **Persistence:** production Redis has persistence enabled and high availability off (`docs/multiplayer.md:228`), and it uses fenced leases (`:232`). Claim only what the tests show: the result survives a reconnect and a gateway replacement in the tested configuration. Make no outage-durability claim. No new database.
- **Retention:** the 30-day default stays configurable. **Blocking decision before any exposure beyond the default-off flag:** Val sets the retention period. Owner deletion must remove the artifact and any derived index in the first slice either way.
- **Rollback:** the flags above. Workplace keys live in their own namespace, so they can be dropped without touching world checkpoints.

**Blocks the first slice:** none of Val's open decisions. The fixture site, draft-only result and default-off flags let #217–#219 proceed.  
**Blocks hosted enablement:** retention default, site choice, an accessibility human review, and #221 evidence.  
**Blocks a remote provider:** provider and spend approval, credential isolation, and a background-execution host.
