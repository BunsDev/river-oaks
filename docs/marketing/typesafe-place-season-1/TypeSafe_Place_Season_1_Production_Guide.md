## 1. The season

**Logline:** Jevica invites the gods to help host a small Halloween open house. She wants everyone to feel included, so she agrees to incompatible plans. Fixing the guest list is easy compared with getting Athena, Dionysus, and Ares to share an evening—and letting herself enjoy it.

**The recurring question:** Can people with very different ideas of a good life make a place they all want to return to?

**The ending:** They can. The welcome becomes kinder, the activities become optional, and the hosts become a team. Jevica finally dances. Afterward, Ares follows up on Athena's offer of help, and their voluntary practice session gives one relationship a next step. Jevica closes the season in a guest chair, with empty hands and a little more trust.

The party is worthwhile even for somebody who only dances and goes home happy. Athena's preparation and Dionysus's delight both matter. The season never requires everyone to turn relaxation into work.

## 3. Publishing rhythm

**One core vertical episode each day, uploaded separately to Instagram and TikTok.** This means **27 creative episodes and 54 planned platform uploads**. Optional Stories provide a second, lighter touchpoint; they are not additional required Reels.

The working account assumption is Jevica's existing Instagram and TikTok presence, with TypeSafe Place clearly identified as the series world. Exact handles are not assumed. If the brand accounts become the primary home, keep this single story sequence and adapt first-person captions into third person. Do not create a separate daily workload for every character.

The original weekly pattern was:

- **Four story episodes:** a conflict, a decision, and a visible consequence.
- **Two light episodes:** a confessional, reaction, dance loop or reusable character moment.
- **One recap:** a clear entry point for viewers who found the series midstream.

The October 12 relaunch cut three light episodes (Days 3, 10 and 17), so weeks no longer follow that pattern exactly. Days 29 and 30 add closure and an inexpensive sequel button. Across the season, that is **17 story clips, 6 light clips and 4 recaps**. Only the party episode is marked Hero; the other story clips rely on short, controlled setups.

### Proposed publishing hour

The workbook starts every post at **6:30 p.m. America/Chicago**. This is an editable working hypothesis, not a known optimum for either account. Keep the first week consistent so posting itself is manageable, then use account evidence to choose a second test.

Chicago is UTC−05:00 through October 31 in this calendar and UTC−06:00 from November 1. Use the named timezone in any later scheduler, so the local time remains 6:30 p.m.

The season launches October 12. Produce the opening posts (Days 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6) as a launch pack, and lock all six character voices first, because all six speak in week one. Go/no-go: Day 1 final in both platform versions by noon October 12. If launch slips to October 13, cut Day 5 next; if it slips further, use the masquerade fallback below. Build toward a three-post buffer first; a seven-post buffer is the steady-state goal, not a claim that the footage already exists. Edit due dates in the workbook are the post date minus three days; the launch pack is due October 12–13, and Day 23 is due October 25 so the season payoff is finished early. If the launch date moves again, move dependent story episodes together. If Halloween no longer fits, change the fictional occasion to a masquerade and revise the dated cards and captions.

## 5. A production system you can sustain

### Concentrate the work in reusable setups

Use the existing visual direction to establish three production setups:

1. **The shared confessional:** charcoal chair, pale travertine wall panels, smoked-bronze trim, dusty-pink practical on image left, courtyard through glass on image right. Match the actual approved booth frame when it is supplied.
2. **The lounge:** one primary social environment. A quiet corner and the entrance are different angles of the same space.
3. **The dance area:** use the lounge's party lighting and a few controlled shots. Preserve the same architecture and approved costumes.

Do not solve every new episode by inventing a new room, wardrobe, companion creature or camera style. The repetition gives the fictional place a recognizable identity and makes the batch easier to produce.

### Work in batches, publish in order

A practical weekly batch has four passes:

| Pass | Work | Ready when |
|---|---|---|
| Writing lock | Review the next seven briefs; make any changes together | The week's cause-and-effect chain and character voices are clear |
| Character and room lock | Choose the exact identity, costume and environment references | A reference frame for each needed setup is accepted |
| Footage and voice | Render the story shots, record or generate the authorized voices, collect light takes | Dialogue is intelligible and every named character matches their reference |
| Editing and packaging | Assemble seven clips, caption, mix cleared sound, export platform versions | The week's clips are ready to preview and publish |

Batch matching shots by character and location where that helps. For example, record Athena's confessionals together, but keep each episode's emotional context distinct. Assemble the episodes in calendar order afterward.

### Use a small render unit

Most story clips should be assembled from **two to four short shots**. One principal character per speaking shot is the default; two named characters in a shot is the planning ceiling. The party uses more cuts, not a single generation attempting six exact faces and simultaneous choreography.

A director brief in this package describes the full edit. For an individual render request, select one shot and include only that shot's action and spoken line. Use a duration supported by the chosen model. If a line is too long, split it at a natural pause instead of asking the model to rush or overlap speakers.

For a difficult story shot, use the same character's confessional as a fallback: narrate the decision, show a clear editor-built story card if necessary, then retain the episode's consequence. The plot should survive a simpler camera setup.

### Reusable single-shot prompt

> Create one vertical 9:16 photorealistic shot for the fictional TypeSafe Place series. Use the selected adult character's supplied reference as the identity and wardrobe authority. Use the approved room reference only for the environment, framing and lighting.\
>
> Episode and chosen shot: [copy the selected shot from today's brief].\
> Action and emotional change: [copy the relevant performance note].\
> Spoken line, if any: [only the dialogue assigned to this shot].\
>
> Preserve the exact face, hair, skin tone, body proportions, clothing coverage, jewelry and signature details. Keep the camera at a natural eye level with restrained motion. One speaker at a time; at most two named characters visible. Natural skin, cloth and wing behavior. No face blending, wardrobe transfer, text generation or added accessories. Leave room for captions and platform controls. Generate no commercial song. Keep existing AI provenance. The result is a fictional series shot, not footage of working gameplay.

### Edit for a viewer who has never met these characters

Use the strongest line or expression immediately. Add a small character name tag on that person's first appearance when useful. Introduce just enough context to understand the local conflict: “We gave Ares the door” is clearer than assuming the viewer watched the rehearsal.

A typical 20–30 second story edit has an immediate hook, a short conflict, a turn, and a reaction or reason to continue. Light clips can be 10–16 seconds. The planned 40-second party has room to pay off multiple characters. These lengths are creative choices, not performance guarantees.

Each brief marks its **Hook delivery**. *Spoken* means the opening line is the hook, so no text overlay is needed. *On-screen* means the hook appears as text over the first two seconds, then the scene plays. Never show on-screen hook text that nearly repeats a different spoken line.

Put the episode number quietly on the cover or caption. Make the spoken hook about the situation, rather than opening every clip by announcing “Episode 17.”

### Audio and text

Use recurring original or properly licensed musical motifs for comedy, anticipation and warmth. Keep dialogue/effects and music separate. Dialogue should remain easy to understand on a phone speaker. Athena can be fast without becoming unintelligible; split her lines into shorter shots when needed.

Create readable invitations, chat cards and captions in the editor. Do not depend on a video model to produce exact phone text. All scripted questions and chat messages in these episodes are fictional props. If a real follower comment later becomes a reply video, use the actual comment and avoid implying the planned fictional question was audience evidence.

## 7. Platform packaging

### Recommended master

Produce an editorial master at **1080 × 1920, vertical 9:16, 30 FPS**, with clear dialogue captions and important faces/text clear of interface controls. TikTok's official Creative Codes provides useful vertical-framing and UI-space advice; it is advertising guidance, and this package does not treat it as a promise about organic reach. [S7]

Export each platform version from the editing project. Use the Instagram caption on Instagram and the shorter conversational caption on TikTok. Maintain the same plot, take selection and character continuity. Make covers readable at a small size with one main face and a short title; use restrained TypeSafe pink #F486A1.

### AI disclosure

These episodes are photorealistic synthetic media. TikTok requires creators to label realistic AI-generated content. Meta describes required disclosure for photorealistic video or realistic-sounding digitally created/altered audio, with its relevant label called “AI info.” Enable the appropriate native disclosure when uploading each episode. [S1–S3]

Every caption ends with one line: “AI-made fictional series 🩷 #TypeSafePlace #TheGuestList.” The assistant episodes (Days 8 and 22) add “(the assistant too)” and Day 27 adds “(the companion too)”, so no scene reads as a real product. The caption line explains the series to people; it accompanies the native disclosure controls and never replaces them.

### Music for both platforms

For promotional TikTok content, use the Commercial Music Library or a separate clearance covering the intended use. TikTok's guidance explicitly includes organic business content among commercial activities. Instagram's ordinary licensed music library is intended for personal, noncommercial use. Meta Sound Collection terms describe use on Meta products. [S4–S6]

Accordingly, choose a cleared soundtrack for each platform separately, or use an original/commissioned/licensed score with explicit rights for both and for the intended promotional use. An Instagram track's availability does not establish permission to carry the exported mixed soundtrack to TikTok. The past “Nicole Kidman” dance idea is a creative reference, not this calendar's automatically cleared soundtrack.

Check whether the post's actual promotional purpose calls for the platform's commercial-content disclosure during publishing. This package does not make paid-ad placements or third-party endorsement commitments.

## 8. Learn from the first month

The workbook's Results tab has one row per episode, platform and capture window: **108 planned snapshots** across 24-hour and 7-day windows. Leave unpublished or unavailable metrics blank; blank does not mean zero.

Capture the measures that the account actually exposes: views, average watch time, completed-view percentage if reported, shares, saves/favorites, comments and attributed follows if available. Record one substantive observation about audience questions or character affinity. A repeated request for Athena/Ares scenes is more useful than a vague note that the post “did well.”

Do not call average watch time divided by video duration a completion rate. They are different measures, and loops or replays can distort that ratio. Keep Instagram and TikTok comparisons separate; compare clips of similar length and format at similar ages after publishing.

Review on Days 7, 14, 21 and 30 using the snapshots available then. The last post's 24-hour observation falls on November 8 and its 7-day observation on November 14 if these dates hold. A Day 30 review cannot contain a full week's results for the newest clips.

Choose one controlled next adjustment at a time: first-frame clarity, dialogue density, a shorter edit, or the posting window. Protect the main story payoffs. Use flexible light slots for a reply, reaction or dance variation, and keep recap references accurate after any swap. Viewer preferences can guide Season 2 without promising that a poll mechanically decides canon.

TikTok Studio describes viewer activity times and account/video analytics, which can help revisit the initial 6:30 p.m. assumption. Account data was not accessed for this plan. [S8]
