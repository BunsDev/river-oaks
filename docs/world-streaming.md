## Ownership and limits

| Resource or policy | Current behavior |
| --- | --- |
| District presentation | Resident; streamed cleanup never disposes its shared resources |
| Commons presentation | One owned group; dynamic module import followed by fresh geometry construction |
| Resident chunks / physical pending loads | At most one of each for the Commons adapter |
| Owned buffer ceiling | 1 MiB; exact unique geometry-array buffers, excluding GPU overhead, materials and textures |
| Prefetch / retention | 85 m / 110 m from the location bounds; local player and camera are anchors |
| Visible destination | A camera-frustum intersection also retains the chunk, even beyond the distance threshold |
| Retry | Exponential delay from 1 s to at most 30 s |
| Load timeout | 15 s; an unresolved import remains counted against physical pending capacity |
| World replacement | Dispose the stream before tearing down the previous world group |

The module cache may retain the imported JavaScript after eviction. Eviction removes the group and disposes its owned geometries and materials; the next visit reconstructs those resources without fetching a second successful module. Current Commons geometry has no textures. Extend byte accounting before adding textures, render targets or other owned allocations; the existing buffer ceiling is not a total-memory guarantee.

Bayou Heights, Studewood, the Bayou Terraces, Woodland Heights and TypeSafe Place stream through the same scheduler as twenty chunks under one 16 MiB geometry ceiling (`preview/src/heights-stream.js`). Each city-kit neighborhood also owns textures it builds and disposes itself: its sign atlas (1024 px by 128 px per distinct sign, mipmapped), tower lettering and tree grate; `render.js` marks them `userData.owned`, and the stream reports their bytes as `measuredTextureBytes` and `residentTextureBytes` beside the geometry figures. The atlases can outweigh a neighborhood's geometry (Nineteenth and Yale: 20 signs, about 13.3 MiB against a 3.45 MiB geometry estimate), so the geometry ceiling is not a total-memory bound; the texture figures are the measured input for deciding a total ceiling, and admission is unchanged until that decision. On the Terraces revisions, where the terraces meet the District edge, District points anchor nothing in Heights; once everyone is back in the District the whole presentation streams out, as it does on `bayou-heights-1`, while an update with no valid position still evicts nothing.

The scheduler sorts candidates by distance and then chunk ID, applies resident and byte limits before starting work, and fences stale or cancelled completions. Rapid exit/reentry cannot create additional physical requests while an uncooperative import remains unresolved. A timed-out import reports a stall and permits retreat; it resumes normal retries after settlement. A permanently unresolved browser import cannot be repaired by starting unlimited replacement imports.
